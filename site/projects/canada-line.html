Overview

The Canada Line is a rapid-transit link connecting Vancouver, Richmond, and Vancouver International Airport. Delivered under a public-private partnership with design, build, finance, operate, and maintain obligations, the line opened in 2009 and remains a major regional transit asset.

MCW provided mechanical and electrical consulting services on the Canada Line under the InTransitBC consortium's design lead, supporting station environments, mechanical systems, lighting, and life-safety design across the corridor.

MCW's Role

MCW's scope covered mechanical and electrical engineering for stations and ancillary infrastructure along the Canada Line corridor. Station environments required coordinated ventilation, lighting, communications, and life-safety systems suited to high-volume transit operation, with each station tied into the larger corridor systems.

How DBFOM Was Applied

DBFOM transit P3s carry the longest accountability tail in public-infrastructure delivery — the consortium remains responsible for operational performance for decades after opening. MCW's design choices had to support reliability, maintainability, and lifecycle cost over the operating period, not only first-cost capital metrics.

Why this project matters

This profile is useful for proposals involving major transit assets, station environments, public-private delivery, and lifecycle-focused infrastructure operations.
